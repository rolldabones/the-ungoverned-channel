This ledger is the study's central artifact and its thesis in table form. Each row records a marketed feature, the governance control it displaces, the failure mode that follows, the compensating control a deployer must build and the residual risk that remains after compensation. The claim being tested: **the product's value and its governability are the same quantity with opposite signs.** If a future product version breaks a row (delivers the feature without displacing the control), the row is marked superseded and the thesis is weakened on the record.

## Reading the ledger

Rows 1 through 8 exhibit the same shape: the feature is the displacement. Row 9 is included against the thesis, deliberately: the audit console is a genuine vendor-supplied control, and honesty requires recording it. The 15 October 2026 re-check adds two more vendor-supplied controls against the thesis, recorded in rows 2 and ~~4~~ 11: memory is now enumerable and deletable by an Owner, and the guest and Slack Connect defaults are off. ~~Predictions P2 and P4 in `11` are scored on them.~~ [Corrected 20 October 2026.] Prediction P2 in `11` is scored on row 2. P4 was scored on the **Respond automatically** default recorded in row 4, which is on and so adverse to the deployer: it supports the thesis. The summary judgment of the ledger: a deployer can zero out most failure modes only by declining features 2, 3, 4 and half of 5, at which point the product purchased is materially not the product marketed. That delta is the **governance discount** defined in `01`, and it is the number a board should ask for before approving deployment.

Final Liability rests with the Human.
